% Introduction

Japan's anime industry is experiencing unprecedented commercial success.
The domestic anime production market reached a record \textyen 362.1 billion
in 2024~\cite{tdb2025anime}, and the global anime market is projected to
grow at a 10.6\% compound annual growth rate, reaching USD~49.6 billion
by 2031~\cite{mordor2026anime}.
Yet behind this growth lies a persistent structural problem:
the creative workforce that produces anime remains largely invisible.

An exceptionally large share of anime production labor is performed by freelancers.
According to the Japan Animation Creators Association (JAniCA),
47.3\% of animation creators were self-employed in 2023~\cite{janica2023survey}---
approximately six times the national average.
These freelancers operate within a multi-layered subcontracting system
where studios routinely outsource entire episodes or specific production tasks
to secondary and tertiary subcontractors.
In this system, individual animators are frequently uncredited:
only the subcontracting studio's name may appear in the final credits,
or no attribution is given at all.

This \textit{contribution invisibility problem} has concrete consequences.
Freelance animators cannot verifiably demonstrate their work history
to prospective clients, weakening their bargaining position
in an industry where 37.7\% of workers earn below \textyen 200{,}000
per month~\cite{nafca2024labor}.
When studios close---a frequent occurrence---the informal records
of who worked on which production disappear entirely.
Recent policy responses, including Japan's Freelance Protection Act (2024)
and the Fair Trade Commission's investigation into anime production
labor practices~\cite{jftc2025anime}, address contractual protections
but do not solve the attribution problem.

In parallel, blockchain technology has introduced new primitives
for decentralized credentialing.
Weyl et al.~\cite{buterin2022decentralized} proposed \textbf{Soulbound Tokens (SBTs)}---
non-transferable tokens bound to a specific blockchain address---
as a mechanism for encoding credentials, commitments, and affiliations
without the speculative dynamics of conventional NFTs.
SBTs have since been applied to healthcare credentialing,
academic certificate verification, and agricultural supply chains,
but \textbf{no prior work has applied SBTs to creative production workflows
or to the credentialing of freelance animators.}

Notably, existing blockchain applications in the anime industry have focused
almost exclusively on \textit{fan-facing} monetization:
IP tokenization, NFT collectibles, and fan engagement platforms
designed to extract value from audiences~\cite{fourpillars2025anime,animechain2024whitepaper}.
In contrast, this paper uses blockchain as a \textit{worker protection} tool---not
to generate revenue from fans, but to safeguard individual creators'
professional identity and career records.

This paper presents \textbf{AnimatorSBT}, a blockchain-based contribution
certification framework designed to integrate SBT issuance into an existing
production management tool for freelance animators.
The system creates immutable, verifiable, and portable records
of individual contributions at the granular level of production tasks
(e.g., key animation cuts, in-between frames, coloring assignments).
Critically, the framework is designed to be \textit{transparent to the end user}:
animators continue their normal workflow of recording tasks in the management tool,
and contribution certificates are issued automatically
upon project milestones without requiring blockchain knowledge,
wallet management, or gas fee payment.

Our contributions are as follows:

\begin{enumerate}
  \item We formalize the \textbf{contribution invisibility problem}
    in the anime industry, defining it as the gap between the set
    of actual contributors and the set of credited contributors,
    and establish five requirements for an ideal solution
    (\cref{sec:problem}).
  \item We propose the \textbf{AnimatorSBT framework},
    consisting of an SBT smart contract, an issuance service,
    and a verification portal integrated with a production management tool
    (\cref{sec:framework}).
  \item We present a \textbf{working prototype deployed on the Polygon Amoy testnet},
    including the smart contract, an issuance service, and a verification portal.
    Cost simulation using measured gas costs shows that full industry adoption
    would cost approximately \textbf{\$256 per year}---$0.000011\%$
    of the industry's annual revenue (\cref{sec:evaluation}).
  \item We identify \textbf{adoption barriers and abuse vectors},
    including studio cooperation challenges, self-reporting risks,
    and regulatory considerations specific to Japan,
    and propose mitigation strategies (\cref{sec:discussion}).
\end{enumerate}

The remainder of this paper is organized as follows.
\Cref{sec:related_work} surveys related work across anime production,
labor conditions, SBTs, and blockchain in the creative economy.
\Cref{sec:problem} formalizes the problem and evaluates existing solutions.
\Cref{sec:framework} describes the AnimatorSBT framework.
\Cref{sec:implementation} presents the technical specification and prototype implementation.
\Cref{sec:evaluation} presents the cost simulation and requirements evaluation.
\Cref{sec:discussion} discusses practical challenges and generalizability.
\Cref{sec:conclusion} concludes with directions for future work.
