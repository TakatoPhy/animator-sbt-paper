% Evaluation

We evaluate AnimatorSBT along three dimensions:
economic viability through cost simulation (\cref{subsec:cost_sim}),
scalability analysis (\cref{subsec:scalability}),
and a qualitative assessment against the requirements defined in \cref{subsec:formalization}.

% ----- 6.1 Cost Simulation -----
\subsection{Cost Simulation}
\label{subsec:cost_sim}

We simulated the annual operating cost of AnimatorSBT across five adoption scenarios,
ranging from a single-studio pilot to full industry adoption.
The simulation uses industry statistics from JAniCA~(2023) and NAFCA~(2024)
for workforce parameters, and gas costs measured on the Polygon PoS testnet
(30~gwei gas price, MATIC = \$0.50).

\subsubsection{Parameters}

The simulation assumes the following industry parameters based on public data:

\begin{itemize}
  \item Total animation creators: $\sim$5{,}500 (JAniCA 2023 survey base)
  \item Freelance ratio: 47.3\%--69.6\% (JAniCA 2023 / 2019)
  \item Average projects per animator per year: 6
  \item Batch minting with batch size of 10 (default)
  \item Revocation rate: 1\% of issued SBTs
  \item IPFS pinning: Free tier for $\leq$500 files; \$20/month for larger deployments
\end{itemize}

The results reveal a striking finding:
\textbf{even at full industry adoption (all freelance animators in Japan),
the total annual operating cost is approximately \$274}---less than
the cost of a single dinner in Tokyo.
This represents $0.000011\%$ of the anime production industry's
annual revenue of \textyen 362.1 billion (\$2.41 billion)~\cite{tdb2025anime}.

The cost is dominated by IPFS pinning fees (\$240/year),
not blockchain transaction costs.
On-chain minting costs account for only \$34 at full adoption,
confirming that Layer~2 blockchains have reduced gas fees
to a point where per-transaction costs are economically negligible.

The per-animator cost decreases with scale due to fixed IPFS costs
being distributed across more users.
At full adoption, the cost is approximately \$0.07 per animator per year---
equivalent to less than one minute of work at the median hourly wage
of \textyen 1{,}111~\cite{nafca2024labor}.

% ----- 6.2 Scalability -----
\subsection{Scalability Analysis}
\label{subsec:scalability}

\subsubsection{Transaction Throughput}

Polygon PoS processes approximately 65{,}000 transactions per day
with a block time of 2 seconds.
At full adoption, AnimatorSBT would generate approximately 22{,}962 minting
transactions per year, or roughly 63 per day.
This represents less than 0.1\% of Polygon's daily capacity,
indicating that the system operates well within the network's limits.

\subsubsection{Storage}

Each SBT metadata JSON file is approximately 500 bytes.
At full adoption, annual metadata storage is approximately
$22{,}962 \times 500 = 11.5$~MB---trivially small for IPFS.
On-chain storage is minimal (token ID, owner address, URI string),
at approximately 200 bytes per token.

\subsubsection{Growth Projection}

Assuming a 10.6\% CAGR in the anime market~\cite{mordor2026anime}
and proportional workforce growth,
the system would need to handle approximately 42{,}000 SBTs per year by 2031
(roughly 115 per day).
This remains well within Polygon's capacity
and would cost approximately \$300--400 annually.

% ----- 6.3 Requirements Evaluation -----
\subsection{Requirements Satisfaction}
\label{subsec:requirements_eval}

We evaluate AnimatorSBT against the five requirements
defined in \cref{subsec:formalization}:

\begin{itemize}
  \item \textbf{R1 (Completeness):} \textit{Satisfied.}
    Any animator using the PMC can receive an SBT regardless of their
    position in the subcontracting hierarchy.
    The issuance is triggered by work log data in the PMC,
    not by studio approval or credit committee decisions.

  \item \textbf{R2 (Verifiability):} \textit{Satisfied.}
    Third parties can verify SBTs by querying the public blockchain.
    The \texttt{evidence\_hash} provides a cryptographic link
    to the underlying work log data.
    No contact with the original studio is required.

  \item \textbf{R3 (Persistence):} \textit{Satisfied.}
    On-chain data persists as long as the Polygon network operates.
    IPFS metadata is content-addressed and can be re-pinned by any party
    with a copy, surviving individual service shutdowns.
    Even if TOONIQ ceases operations, the on-chain records remain accessible.

  \item \textbf{R4 (Portability):} \textit{Satisfied.}
    The SBT is bound to the animator's wallet address,
    which they control via account abstraction.
    The animator can present their wallet address (or a derived identifier)
    to any verifier, independent of any specific platform.

  \item \textbf{R5 (Minimal Friction):} \textit{Satisfied.}
    The animator's workflow is unchanged---they continue recording work
    in the PMC as before.
    SBT issuance is automatic upon project milestones.
    No wallet management, gas fee payment, or blockchain knowledge is required.
\end{itemize}

% ----- 6.4 Limitations -----
\subsection{Limitations}
\label{subsec:eval_limitations}

The current evaluation has several limitations that should be addressed
in future work:

\begin{itemize}
  \item \textbf{No user study:}
    We have not yet conducted a formal user study with practicing animators.
    The cost simulation and requirements analysis provide theoretical validation,
    but real-world usability and perceived value remain to be evaluated.
  \item \textbf{Gas price volatility:}
    The cost estimates assume a fixed gas price of 30~gwei and MATIC price of \$0.50.
    While Polygon PoS gas prices are historically stable,
    significant price fluctuations could affect cost projections.
  \item \textbf{Self-reported data:}
    The SBT metadata is derived from self-reported work logs in the PMC.
    Without a co-signing mechanism (e.g., studio confirmation),
    the system cannot prevent fabricated claims.
    We discuss potential mitigation strategies in \cref{sec:discussion}.
  \item \textbf{Adoption assumptions:}
    The simulation assumes uniform adoption across the industry.
    In practice, adoption will be uneven and influenced by
    network effects, studio policies, and cultural factors.
\end{itemize}
