% Abstract

Japan's anime production market reached a record \textyen 362.1 billion (approximately US\$2.4 billion at \textyen 150/US\$) in 2024, yet the freelance animators who make up roughly half of the production workforce (47.3\% self-employed in 2023, and up to 69.6\% by 2019 estimates) remain largely invisible in official credits due to subcontracting hierarchies, staff turnover, and limited credit slots. This paper proposes \textbf{AnimatorSBT}, a blockchain-based contribution certification framework that leverages Soulbound Tokens (SBTs)---non-transferable tokens introduced by Weyl et al.~(2022)---to create immutable, tamper-evident records of animators' work contributions at the granular level of individual production tasks. The framework is designed for integration with an existing production management tool for freelance animators, targeting the Polygon PoS blockchain for deployment. A prototype---comprising the smart contract deployed on the Polygon Amoy testnet, an issuance service, and a verification portal---demonstrates technical feasibility and on-chain correctness, with gas costs measured on-chain. We additionally provide a reference implementation of the studio co-signing (authenticity) layer and a deterministic ``canonical'' evidence-hash serialization as a separate contract and module, validated by a 60-test suite, though these are not yet deployed on-chain or operated with independent studios. A cost analysis shows that the marginal cost is low: on-chain minting is approximately \$0.0013 per self-attested certificate and \$0.0019 with a studio co-signature, while the dominant operating cost is a flat IPFS pinning subscription of roughly \$240/year, a centralized off-chain dependency. We assess the design against five requirements for an ideal credentialing solution---completeness, verifiability, persistence, portability, and minimal friction---and argue it meets them by design, with two important caveats: \textit{verifiability} is currently limited to integrity and existence (the authenticity of self-reported claims requires a studio co-signing layer that we design and implement as a tested reference contract, but do not yet deploy on-chain or operate with independent studio attesters---in the present single-operator configuration the issuer also holds the attester role), and the minimal-friction and portability claims rest on wallet abstraction that is not yet built and remains to be validated with users. Our framework contributes to the emerging literature on decentralized identity for creative gig workers and offers a replicable model for other project-based creative industries.
