% Abstract

Project-based creative production distributes work across layers of
subcontractors and credits a capped subset of the people who did it. Where a union
holds craft jurisdiction an arbitration procedure decides attribution; where none
does, standards without adjudicators leave contributors uncredited at measurable
rates. Japanese animation is the second case: 37.0\% of animation creators describe
themselves as freelance or self-employed, no body holds jurisdiction over crediting, and
the state's own model contract for freelance animators has the animator agree not
to exercise the moral right to be named.

We present AnimatorSBT, a certification framework that issues a non-transferable
token per unit of accepted work, anchored to an order a studio has accepted and
paid for rather than to a self-reported log, so that a co-signature records an
attestation that already occurred instead of manufacturing one. The contract is
deployed on a public testnet; a studio co-signing layer and a deterministic
evidence-hash serialisation are implemented as tested reference components; gas is
measured for every operation and reproducible from the repository.

The cost result is the transferable one. On-chain issuance costs about a tenth of
a cent per certificate, and that figure is governed less by the contract's logic
than by the length of the metadata pointer it stores, so a reported
per-certificate cost is uninterpretable unless the storage scheme is stated. The
binding cost is a flat off-chain pinning subscription, which reintroduces the
dependency on one operator that anchoring on chain was meant to remove.
Authenticity remains unestablished: co-signing is implemented and tested but not
operated with independent studio attesters, so in the present configuration the
issuer also holds the attester role.
